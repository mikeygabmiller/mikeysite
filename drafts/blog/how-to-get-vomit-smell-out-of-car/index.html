How to Get Vomit Smell Out of a Car

What to do in the first hour, the places it hides that people miss, and why the smell comes back on the first warm day.

By Mikey · December 2026

How do you get vomit smell out of a car?

Get it up fast, blot instead of rubbing, treat the spot with an enzyme cleaner and let it dry completely. If the smell comes back on a warm day or when the heater runs, it soaked through to the carpet padding or the seat foam, and that needs hot water extraction to pull it back out. Kids get carsick, someone has a rough night, a dog eats something it shouldn't. No judgment at all. I've seen everything. One of my reviews is from a customer who got sick in her car, did the first cleanup herself, and was worried the smell would stay. I fit her in quickly and extracted the carpets and did the whole car. The sooner it gets dealt with, the easier it comes out, so here's what to do first.

The first hour

  • Gloves on, windows down. Air it out while you work.
  • Lift the solids, don't wipe them. A piece of cardboard or a plastic spatula scoops it up. Wiping spreads it wider and pushes it into the fibers.
  • Blot what's left. Paper towels, pressed down, swapped for clean ones until they come up dry. Rubbing works it deeper into the carpet.
  • Use an enzyme cleaner. The pet odor kind from a pet store. It breaks down what's causing the smell instead of covering it. Spray, wait as long as the label says, blot again.
  • Dry it out. Baking soda on the damp spot pulls moisture and odor. Leave it until it's dry, then vacuum it up. Crack the windows if the car is parked somewhere safe.
  • Skip the air freshener. It just mixes with the smell. You'll know it's really gone when you can't smell anything at all.

Where it hides

The spot you can see is usually not the only spot. Before you call it done, check:

  • The seat belt and the buckle. The one people miss most. The webbing soaks it up and the buckle catches it.
  • The seams and the gap between the seat and the console. It runs down there and sits.
  • Under the seat and in the seat tracks. Slide the seat all the way forward and back and look.
  • Door pockets, the door panel and the vents if it was the front seat.
  • The floor mat and the carpet under it. A rubber mat holds it in a puddle underneath. Pull it out.

If it was in a car seat

Check the car seat's manual before you clean it. Most covers come off, and most manuals say not to soak the harness straps or use strong cleaners on them, because it can weaken them. A crash is not where you want to find that out. More on that in cleaning up after kids.

Why the smell comes back

Carpet in a car sits on a thick layer of padding, and seats are foam under the fabric. A spill that soaks through looks clean on top while the padding underneath is still holding it. Then the sun hits the car, or you run the heater on a cold morning, and it warms up and comes back. That's the sign it's in the padding, and nothing you spray on top will reach it.

What reaches it is hot water extraction: flushing the carpet and the seat through with a cleaning solution and pulling it straight back out with the extractor, so whatever soaked in comes out with the water. Then I sanitize and do the rest of the interior, because a smell doesn't stay in one spot in a closed car. If I think a car will take more than one pass, I tell you that before I start, not after.

The longer it sits, the harder it is. A day or two is usually straightforward. Weeks in a warm car is a bigger job. If it's a smell you can't find the source of, this post covers the other usual causes.
