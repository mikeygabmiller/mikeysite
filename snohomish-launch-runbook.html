Three things to watch

None of these should delay launch. All three are worth a look in week one.

1. The negative on "car detailing" may be throttling your best keyword

The build adds negative-exact car detailing while bidding broad on the same words. The handoff calls this deliberate: the bare head term supposedly converted poorly. But the account doesn't show that. What it shows is that phrase-match "car detailing" converted zero on $54.53 — which is a different thing from the exact query. Meanwhile broad "car detailing" delivered 15 of your 32 keyword conversions at $4.63.

In a five-mile radius with maybe 300–450 searches a month, the bare two-word query is likely a real share of what little volume exists. Since underspending is the predicted failure mode here, this negative is pointed the wrong way. If you're under 20 clicks after week one, remove this negative before you touch anything else. I can do it in one call.

2. Four wasteful terms are still un-negatived

The negative list catches the big ones — mobile automobile detailing alone burned $214.31 for nothing. But car detail near me ($17.20), car detailers near me ($10.13), professional car detailing ($8.59) and affordable auto detailing near me ($8.30) all converted zero and aren't blocked.

I deliberately left them alone. That's $44 across three-to-six clicks each — too thin to act on, and this campaign's bigger risk is starving for volume, not overspending. Revisit only if they show up in your search terms report burning real money.

3. Expect it to underspend, and don't fix that with bids

At $10/day in a five-mile radius, expect $80–120 of the $300 monthly budget to actually deploy. That's the geography, not a broken build. A realistic month is 30–45 clicks and 5–7 conversions.

The decision rule: if two weeks pass with under 20 clicks total, widen the radius to 15 miles — that picks up Monroe, Lake Stevens and Everett. Do not raise bids. Bidding harder into inventory that doesn't exist just costs more per click for the same handful of clicks.
