# SEO R&D track 2: the map pack (2026-10-27)

Question: where does Mikey's Mobile Detailing show in Google's map pack across
the twelve towns, why does it lose where it loses, and what would move it?
Research only. Nothing on the profile was changed, nothing was reported, no
review was requested.

**The short answer.** On 2026-10-27, across 21 points in the twelve towns and
four searches, the profile was **never in the top 3**. Its best spot was 4th
for "mobile detailing" east of Snohomish (Three Lakes). Downtown Snohomish it
was 24th to 29th. In 8 of the 12 towns it wasn't in the top 60 for any of the
four searches (wide view). The one place it wins is a search that names the town: "mobile
detailing snohomish" puts it **2nd** from downtown Snohomish, Lake Stevens and
Monroe.

The reason is mostly not reviews. **Of 3,360 top-20 slots in the grid, 73
(2.2%) went to a listing with a hidden address.** Every top-3 slot but two
went to a listing that shows a street address, and the top-3 winners sat a
median **2.0 miles** from the searcher. A 5.0 profile with 40 reviews and a
hidden address loses to a listing with **zero reviews** and a visible
Snohomish address (Scott's Good Detailing, 1st in downtown Snohomish for all
four searches). Whitespark's 2026 survey ranks "address is showing (not SAB)"
the 7th most important map pack factor, and Sterling Sky's test (republished
2026-09-22) saw rankings drop when an address was hidden and come back when it
was shown.

So the plan's map pack goal (SG2: top 3 from 8 of 12 grid points) can't be
reached by profile work, and part of GROWTH-PLAN.md's Phase 2 is out of date or
against Google's rules (section 6). The realistic target is the area around his
base plus searches that name a town; the far towns are a city-page, organic and
Ads job.

The biggest lever in the data is **showing an address**, and it isn't on the
list on purpose. Google's rule is that a business that doesn't serve customers
at its address hides it ("If you're a service-area business, you should hide
your business address from customers", Google Business Profile guidelines,
read 2026-10-27). Showing his home would break that rule, risk a suspension of
the profile most of his leads come from, and put his home on the map. It only
becomes a real option if he ever has a place customers actually come to (a
shop or a drop-off spot with a sign). That's a question for him, not a move.

## 3. The proximity ceiling

**Method.** For every search in the grid, the distance between the search
point and each of the top 3 listings' map pins (great-circle).

| Map size | Median distance of a top-3 winner | 75% within | 90% within | Farthest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wide (~12 mi across) | 2.0 mi | 3.2 mi | 4.7 mi | 9.2 mi |
| Tight (~3 mi across) | 2.2 mi | 3.7 mi | 5.3 mi | 17.9 mi |

In this market a listing almost never holds the top 3 more than about 5 miles
from where it's pinned, and that's for listings that show an address. Mikey's
hidden-address listing didn't reach the top 3 at any distance; its best was 4th
at 2.6 miles, where few other detailers are pinned. At 1.5 miles (Machias) it
was 21st, because more competitors are pinned nearby.

What that means for the twelve towns:

- **Winnable in 6 months (top 3 at some points, top 10 at more):** his own
  surroundings, Snohomish east and Machias. Downtown Snohomish and Lake Stevens
  are top-10 territory at best: the top 3 there show addresses within a mile or
  two and include ProDetail (112 reviews) and Lake Stevens Auto Detailing (74).
- **Town-named searches:** already 2nd for "mobile detailing snohomish" from
  three towns. Keep the relevance signals (the profile's name, category,
  services, the site's Snohomish pages) and protect them.
- **Map pack is out of reach; city page, organic and Ads only:** Everett,
  Marysville, Mill Creek, Mukilteo, Bothell, Woodinville, Duvall, Arlington,
  and in practice Monroe and Granite Falls (8.7 and 9.3 miles, 32nd to 39th).
  Google Ads already covers six of these towns (`reports/2026-10-16-muse-ads-locations.md`).
  GROWTH-PLAN.md's G7 line, "every unbuilt city is a Map Pack you're not in",
  is wrong for a service-area business: a city page doesn't move the pin.

This matches GROWTH-PLAN.md's own risk note ("Proximity caps you") but puts
the cap at roughly 2 to 5 miles, not 25.

**Does this square with Search Console?** Mostly. The scoreboard shows the
profile's website button at position 1 for "mobile detailing" (50 impressions)
and "car detailing" (26). Search Console gives every listing in a map pack the
pack's own position, so "1" means "was in a pack that sat at the top", and 50
impressions in three months is a small pocket of searchers, consistent with
the few points near his base where he ranks well.

## 7. Method and its limits

**How the grid was run.** Google web search refused this sandbox (a "unusual
traffic" page on the first request). Google Maps answered. A headless browser
opened Maps, then asked Maps' own search endpoint (`/search?tbm=map`, the
request the Maps page makes) for each search, centred on each point, at two map
sizes, and read results 1 to 60 in order (three pages of 20). This is the same
idea as the paid geogrid trackers (Local Falcon, BrightLocal): a Maps search
pinned to a point. Each result came back with its name, categories, map pin,
whether an address is shown, website and phone. Competitor details (review
count, rating, hours, latest owner post, owner replies) were read from each
public listing page through a page-reading service the same day.

**Limits, plainly:**

1. **It's Google Maps, signed out, in Google's "limited view".** The phone
   map pack on google.com and a signed-in searcher can differ. Rankings from
   the Maps endpoint are what geogrid tools use, but no logged-in or on-the-
   ground check was possible from here.
2. **One day, one run.** Local rankings move day to day; treat any single rank
   as plus or minus a few places. The pattern (hidden address, never top 3,
   2 mi winners) held across all 168 searches.
3. **Points are town centres and in-between spots,** 21 of them, not an even
   grid; good enough to compare towns, not a heat map.
4. **Review velocity wasn't measurable.** The limited view shows only a
   listing's total and three reviews, not the review list by date. So there is
   no reviews-per-month figure for competitors. What was visible: Schmitt's,
   Silver Diamond, Custom Automotive and The Detail Buff each had a review
   marked "New" (last week or two); On The Go had three, Opulence two.
   A second reading of each total in a month gives a true rate.
5. **Not readable in the limited view:** photo counts (except Scott's, 9),
   services lists, how long each profile has existed. Posting frequency only as
   "the latest post": Blue Star 11 hours ago, Mikey 14 hours ago, On The Go 8
   hours ago, Everett Lux 3 days, The Detail Buff 4 days, Opulence Oct 13,
   Burdys Oct 5, APC Oct 2, Schmitt's and Marysville Lux Jul 29, Silver Diamond
   May 12; none seen for the rest.
6. **Semrush** wasn't used: it had no API units left on 2026-10-27 (the SEO
   research report the same day) and its local data covers organic results, not
   the map pack.
7. A request to a remote "stealth" browser to get past the limited view was
   refused by this session's safety check as bot-check circumvention, so it
   wasn't pursued.

**To rerun it monthly** (move 9): the same endpoint, the 21 points in this
section's table, the four searches, 60 results, the two map sizes. A paid
tracker signed in from a real location is better if Mikey wants to pay for one.
