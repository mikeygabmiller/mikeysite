Tree Sap Off Your Car, Without Hurting the Paint

Firs, cedars and pines drop sap on every car parked under them around here. Here's what takes it off safely, what makes it worse, and how to tell when it has already marked the paint.

By Mikey · October 2026

How do you get tree sap off a car without damaging the paint?

Soften it, then lift it. Don't scrape it. Rubbing alcohol or hand sanitizer on a microfiber, held on the sap for a minute, dissolves it so it wipes away. Then wash the spot, because alcohol strips wax. If a mark is still there once the sap is gone, it has etched the clear coat and needs polishing. I'm Mikey, of Mikey's Mobile Detailing in Snohomish, WA, and sap is a weekly job here.

Park under a Douglas fir in Snohomish County for a week and you'll find out. The drops start clear and soft, then go hard and amber, and by then most people reach for a fingernail. That's the step that does the damage. Sap itself comes off fine if you let something dissolve it first.

Sap, or the sticky mist under the maples?

Two different things land on cars around here. Sap comes from evergreens: firs, pines, cedars, spruce. It shows up as separate drops, clear to amber, and it hardens. The fine sticky film you get under maples, birches and lindens in summer usually isn't sap at all. It's honeydew, dripped by aphids feeding in the tree. Honeydew is sugary, so it washes off with car soap and warm water. Sap won't, which is why the rest of this post is about sap.

How to get it off

  • Wash the car first, or at least the area. Grit around a sap drop turns your towel into sandpaper.
  • Soak a folded microfiber in rubbing alcohol (70% is fine) or a squirt of hand sanitizer, and hold it on the drop for a minute or two. You're dissolving it, not scrubbing it.
  • Lift, don't rub. Wipe gently in one direction. Hard drops may need two or three rounds of soaking. Patience is the whole trick.
  • Wash the spot again, then put a coat of spray wax or sealant on it. Alcohol takes your protection off along with the sap.

A bug and tar remover made for car paint works too, and it's gentler on wax. Read the label and keep it off plastic trim if it says to.

On glass, a new razor blade held flat with soapy water takes hardened sap off quickly. Glass only: never paint, plastic or the inside of a tinted window.

What not to use

  • Fingernails, scrapers, the rough side of a sponge. They take clear coat with the sap.
  • Lacquer thinner, acetone or nail polish remover. They can soften clear coat and wreck plastic trim.
  • A heat gun. Hot paint scratches easier, and it's easy to overheat a spot.
  • Waiting until spring. The longer sap sits, the harder it gets, and in summer sun it can etch.

When it leaves a mark

Sometimes the sap comes off and a dull spot or ring stays behind, the shape of the drop. That's the clear coat itself, etched while the sap sat on it. No cleaner fixes that, because nothing is left on top of the paint to clean. It needs machine polishing to level the clear coat around the mark, the same way a light scratch comes out. (If you're not sure whether a mark is in the paint or on it, the fingernail test works here too.)

Keeping it off

You can't always move the car. What helps is a slick coat on the paint, so sap sits on top of the protection instead of bonding to the clear coat, and comes off easier. Wax lasts weeks, a sealant a few months, a ceramic coating years. And check under the trees every week or two in spring and summer, when the drops are still soft.

The needles that come with the sap matter too. They pile up in the cowl, the slot under the wipers, and plug the drains. That water can end up on your floor.
