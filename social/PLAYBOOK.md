# Instagram + Facebook playbook

How Mikey's Mobile Detailing posts on Instagram and Facebook: what to post,
how often, what the captions sound like, what the offer is, and how to keep it
going without it eating your week.

Everything that's ready to post is in this folder:

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| The images | `posts/` (1080×1350 JPGs, Instagram's tallest shape, Facebook shows it uncropped) |
| Every caption, Instagram and Facebook versions, plus alt text | `CAPTIONS.md` |
| Profile picture and Facebook cover | `posts/profile-picture.jpg`, `posts/facebook-cover.jpg` |
| Photos with plates and house numbers blurred | `photos/` |
| The generator that makes all of the above | `tools/` (see "Making more posts" at the bottom) |

This folder is not published on the website (`_config.yml` excludes it).

---

## 1. What to expect (read this first)

A brand new page with zero followers will not fill your calendar on its own for
a while. For the first few months the page has one main job: **proof.** People
who hear about you from a neighbor, Nextdoor or Google will look you up before
they let a stranger work in their driveway. A page full of real before and
afters, real reviews and a real guy answers that question.

The things that actually reach new people, in order:

1. **Reels** (short vertical videos). Instagram shows these to people who don't
   follow you. Photos mostly reach people who already do. See section 7.
2. **Being helpful in each town's Facebook community group** when someone asks
   for a detailer. See section 9.
3. **Consistency.** Three posts a week for three months beats twenty posts in
   week one and nothing after.

## 2. Setting up the accounts

Do Facebook first, then Instagram, then link them. Menu names move around in
these apps; if a step doesn't match exactly, look for the nearest wording.

### Facebook Page

1. From your personal Facebook: Menu → **Pages** → **Create new Page**.
2. **Name:** `Mikey's Mobile Detailing` (exactly this, same as Google, so the
   listings match).
3. **Category:** type "detail" and pick the closest match (car detailing,
   auto detailing, or "Car Wash" if nothing closer comes up).
4. **Bio** (under 101 characters):
   > Mobile car detailing in your driveway around Snohomish County. You don't pay until you love it.
5. **Contact:** phone `(425) 600-7897`, website
   `https://mikeysdetailing.com/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=page`
   (the tail on the link tells your Google Analytics the visit came from Facebook).
6. **Location:** do NOT put your home address on the page. Choose a service
   area instead and add the 12 towns (section 5, post P09 has the list).
7. **Hours:** leave them off for now. We haven't confirmed which days you work,
   and wrong hours are worse than none.
8. **Profile picture:** `posts/profile-picture.jpg`. **Cover photo:**
   `posts/facebook-cover.jpg`. The cover keeps everything in the middle, so
   phones (which crop the sides) still show the whole logo.

### Instagram

1. Create the account with username **`@mikeysdetailing`**.
2. Switch it to a business account: Settings → **Account type and tools** →
   **Switch to professional account** → pick the same category → **Business**.
3. **Name field:** `Mikey's Mobile Detailing` (this field is searchable, the
   username alone isn't enough).
4. **Bio** (143 of 150 characters, keep the line breaks):
   ```
   Mobile car detailing in your driveway, Snohomish County.
   300+ cars, 5.0 on Google.
   You don't pay until you love it.
   Exact price in 60 sec below
   ```
5. **Link:** `https://mikeysdetailing.com/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio`
6. **Profile picture:** `posts/profile-picture.jpg` (just the car from the logo;
   the full logo is unreadable in a circle that small).
7. **Link Instagram to the Facebook Page** (Instagram: Settings → Accounts
   Center, or Facebook Page settings → Linked accounts → Instagram). This is
   what lets you schedule both from one place.

### Scheduling tool

**Meta Business Suite** (free, `business.facebook.com` or the Meta Business
Suite app). Once the accounts are linked you can load a whole month in one
sitting: Planner → Create post → tick both Facebook and Instagram → add the
images → paste the caption → Schedule. Turn on the option to write different
text for each platform, since the two captions differ (Instagram says "link in
bio", Facebook has the actual link and no hashtags).

## 3. The offer: the Rain-Ready Full Detail

> **Book a full detail between October and March and every extra on my menu
> comes free:** exterior polish ($30), ceramic wax ($20), RainX on the windows
> ($10), carpet shampoo ($20). **$80 of extras.** To get it, type **RAIN READY**
> in the notes box of the quote, or text it to (425) 600-7897.

It's bank post **B07**. It does **not** go up at launch: people who've never
heard of you don't want an offer from you yet. Post it no earlier than week 5,
after a month of tips, and never right after another ask.

### ⚠️ Confirm this before B07 goes up

This is a real promise to real customers, so it's the one thing in here that
needs your yes. What it costs you: nothing off the full detail price, roughly
an extra hour of work per offer car (polish is most of that), and a little
product. If that's not OK, just never post B07. Nothing else depends on it.

### Why this offer (the Hormozi logic)

Hormozi's rule is to make the offer so good people feel dumb saying no, **by
adding value, not cutting price.** His value equation:

> value = (the result they want × how sure they are they'll get it) ÷ (how long it takes × how much hassle it is)

| Lever | How this offer pulls it |
|---|---|
| The result they want | "My car is ready for six months of rain." A named, seasonal goal instead of "a detail." |
| How sure they are | 5.0 across 40 Google reviews, 300+ cars, and the guarantee: walk-around at the end, fix it on the spot, come back free, full refund if still not happy. |
| How long it takes | Exact price in 60 seconds, a text back in minutes, done in one visit. |
| How much hassle | You come to them. They don't have to be home. |

The rest of his checklist:

- **Bonuses, not a discount.** Knocking $80 off tells people your price was
  padded. Giving $80 of extras keeps the full detail price whole and makes it
  look like more.
- **Cheap for you to deliver, valuable to them.** All four are things you
  already carry and already sell. Each one solves a rain-season problem: dull
  paint, water sitting on the paint, a windshield you can't see through,
  muddy carpets.
- **Real scarcity:** 12 cars a week. It's the one scarcity claim the site
  makes, so it's the only one the posts make.
- **Real urgency:** the rain season window. Named, not a fake countdown. The
  window really ends, so the second visit to the page doesn't make it look
  fake.
- **A name that says who it's for and what it gets them.** "Rain-Ready Full
  Detail" beats "Fall Special."
- **A code word.** RAIN READY lands in the notes of the quote (the same notes
  line that reaches your texts and email), so you can count exactly how many
  bookings came from social.

It's also deliberately smaller than the old free exterior offer. That one gave
away a whole $160 service. This one gives away $80 of extras on your
highest-priced job.

### Honoring it

- Applies to a **Full Detail** booked October 1 to March 31 where the customer
  wrote or texted **RAIN READY**.
- If they also ticked some of the extras in the quote calculator, don't charge
  for those extras.
- Busy weeks: don't quietly drop the polish. If the offer gets more popular
  than your schedule can take, post that it's closed and stop posting B07.
- **After March 31, stop posting it.** For summer, the same four-extra bundle
  can come back as a "Road-Trip Ready Full Detail" (bugs, tree sap, summer
  dust) if you want it.

## 4. What to post: give, give, give, then ask

Hormozi's rule for content, and yours: **give so much value for free that
people feel like they owe you.** A stranger should get something useful out of
every post even if they never book. The selling happens in your bio and when
they come to you, not in the feed.

| Type | What it is | Share of posts |
|---|---|---|
| **Teach** | A tip or technique they can use in their own driveway: washing, pet hair, spills, smells, glass | Most of them |
| **Myths** | "Everyone says X. Here's what actually happens." | About one in eight |
| **Story** | Something that happened on a job, told the way you'd tell a friend. Before/afters go here, as the story of that car | One a week once you've answered the story questions |
| **Ask** | The offer, the guarantee, how booking works, reviews, the map | **At most one in four**, never two in a row |

**Every value post ends with "save this" or a question, not a quote link.**
Saves and comments are what Instagram uses to decide who else sees a post,
and a question gets people talking to you, which is where bookings start.

**The weekly rhythm:** three posts a week, on three days you can keep (for
example Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday). Evenings tend to be when people scroll.

**Tip posts are carousels** (5 to 7 slides: a hook, one tip per slide, a
sign-off). They're the most saved format on Instagram, and they don't need a
photo, which is why the first month doesn't depend on new pictures.

### Stories: the part only you can write

I won't make up stories, and the good ones are yours anyway. Answer any of
these in the **Story questions** doc in the Mikey Social Photos Drive folder.
Talk-to-text on your phone is fine; a few rough sentences each is plenty. I
turn each answer into a post.

1. The worst car you've ever done. What was in it, and how long did it take?
2. Why did you start detailing in 2021?
3. Something you learned the hard way, a mistake you made early on.
4. A customer who surprised you.
5. The thing people apologize for most when you open their car.
6. Your favorite tool, and why it beats the one everyone buys.
7. A car that fought back: what took the longest to get out?
8. What would you check on a used car before buying it?
9. The rainiest job you've done. How did you make it work?
10. What does a normal day look like, from the first car to the last?

### Posting checklist, every post

1. Images in the order listed in `CAPTIONS.md` (carousels: split first).
2. The right caption for the platform (Instagram or Facebook version).
3. Alt text on Instagram: Advanced settings → Accessibility → Write alt text.

   use the plain photo there rather than the graphic, and drop the hashtags.

   and a question in a DM is a customer asking for a quote: send them the
   link.

## 6. Photos on the job (the thing that keeps this going)

The site's photos are small (680 pixels wide) and only two of them are true
before/afters. The originals on your phone are several times bigger and
sharper. Every job is a chance to refill the bank.

**The before/after routine, about 60 seconds per car:**

1. Shoot the **before** before you touch anything.
2. Hold the phone **vertical**. That's the shape the posts use.
3. Same four spots every car: driver footwell, back seat, cargo area, the
   outside from the front corner.
4. For each spot, notice where you're standing and what's in the corner of the
   frame (a door edge, a seat belt). Stand in the same place, same zoom, for
   the **after**. Matching angles is what makes a before/after hit.
5. Wipe the lens. Avoid shooting straight into the sun (the back seat "after"
   on the site is washed out on one side for that reason).

**Privacy rules (you agreed to these):**

- No readable license plates. Shoot at an angle or I'll blur them.
- No house numbers, street signs or anything that says whose house it is.
- No customer faces, no names, no kids' names on car seats or bags.
- **Ask first:** "Mind if I post before and after pics of the car? No plate,
  no address, no names." Only post if they say yes.

**Getting them to me:** drop them in the Google Drive folder **Mikey Social
Photos**, one folder per job, named like this:

```
2019 MDX - Lake Stevens - full interior - cereal ground into leather
```

The car, town, service and what was wrong is everything a caption needs, and it
means nothing gets made up.

## 7. Reels (short videos)

No talking needed. Vertical, 5 to 15 seconds, text on screen, no plates or
house numbers. Ideas that work for detailing:

1. The extractor pulling dirty water off a seat or carpet.
2. A before/after transition: film the before, then the after from the exact
   same spot, and cut between them.
3. Pet hair coming up with a rubber brush.
4. Water beading on fresh wax when you mist it with the hose.
5. Wiping a door jamb: the dirty towel is the payoff.
6. A slow 10 second walk-around of the finished car.

Film a couple per week while you work. Reels usually reach far more new
people than photo posts do, because Instagram shows them to non-followers.

## 8. Caption rules

Same voice as the website, because it's the same guy:

- **First person.** "I come to you", never "we".
- **Say what happens in the driveway**, not how the customer will feel.
- **Concrete beats hype.** "Crumbs ground into every seam" beats "showroom
  shine". No "seamless", "elevate", "transform", "jaw-dropping".
- **No em dashes.** Full stops, commas, colons or brackets instead.
- **Only claim what the photo shows.** If you don't know which service a car
  got, describe what the service covers, not what was done to that car.
- **The shape:** a first line that makes sense on its own (Instagram cuts the
  rest behind "more"), two to four short lines of what happened, one fact
  (price range, pay after, 60 second quote, 12 cars a week), then the call to
  action. Instagram: "link in bio". Facebook: the actual link.
- **Hashtags:** 3 to 5 on Instagram, mostly local (#snohomishcounty,
  #lakestevens, #everettwa, #monroewa, #millcreekwa, #bothellwa,
  #marysvillewa) plus one or two trade tags (#mobiledetailing,
  #cardetailing). None on Facebook.

**Facts:** only from the table in the repo's `CLAUDE.md`. Two are **not
confirmed** and stay out of every post until you say otherwise: **which days
you work** and **whether you're licensed and insured.**

## 9. Beyond your own page

- **Each town's Facebook community group** (search "Lake Stevens community",
  "Everett community", and so on). Read the rules first: many ban business
  posts but allow replies. When someone asks "anyone know a good detailer?",
  answer as yourself, briefly, with the quote link. Don't paste the same ad
  into every group. That gets you removed and remembered for the wrong reason.
- **Nextdoor:** claim a free business page. Same rule: answer, don't advertise.
- **Ask happy customers to tag you** when they post their car. A customer's
  post reaches people who trust them.

## 10. Keeping score (once a month, 10 minutes)

- **Bookings from social:** count the quotes and texts that said RAIN READY.
- **Site visits from social:** Google Analytics → Reports → Acquisition →
  Traffic acquisition, look for `instagram` and `facebook` as the source
  (that's what the link tails in the bios are for).
- **Which posts worked:** in Instagram's insights, look at **saves, shares
  and DMs**, not likes. Make more of whatever type got those.

## Making more posts (for whoever works on this next, Claude included)

Every post lives in `tools/posts.cjs`: what the image says, the Instagram
caption, and the alt text. The Facebook caption is built from the Instagram
one. Change the copy there, never in `CAPTIONS.md`, which gets rewritten.

```sh
cd social/tools
npm install          # once: sharp + playwright (uses the preinstalled Chromium)
npm run photos       # only when photos changed: re-blurs into social/photos/
npm run render       # all posts, or: node render.cjs P07 B02
```

- **New photo:** add it to `PHOTOS` in `tools/prep-photos.cjs` with blur boxes
  for any plate or house number, run `npm run photos`, and **look at the
  output.** The boxes are drawn by eye.
- **New post:** copy the nearest existing entry in `posts.cjs` and reuse one of
  its templates (`statement`, `split`, `slide`, `fit`, `photocard`, `duo`,
  `grid`, `review`, `offer`, `steps`, `map`). The renderer warns when text
  overflows its box. Look at every image before calling it done.
- **Small photos:** the site's gallery shots are 680px wide. Templates show
  them at or below about 1.4× that; put a small photo in `duo` or `grid`
  (where it's shrunk), never stretched full width.
- **The map** is built from `tools/service-area-map.svg`, with the "ask me"
  towns taken off. Lynnwood and Edmonds are not served and must not appear
  as if they were.
