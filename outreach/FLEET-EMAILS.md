# Fleet and dealership emails

Cold emails to businesses that have a lot of vehicles: service companies with
vans and trucks, used-car lots, franchise dealerships, and employers with a
full parking lot. The goal is regular work, not "a contract": a standing spot,
one vehicle to start, pay after.

This folder is not served (`_config.yml` excludes it). Every email here is one
more copy of the facts in `CLAUDE.md`, so a fact change lands here too.

---

## 1. Before you send a single one

These are the things a business will ask about in its first reply. Have the
answer before the question comes, or the reply is where the deal dies.

- [ ] **Insurance. This is the blocker.** Your own playbook still lists it as
      not done. A dealership will not let you touch inventory without a
      certificate of insurance (COI) showing general liability plus
      **garagekeepers** coverage, usually with the dealership named as
      "additional insured". A plumber with six vans may not ask, but if you
      scratch a $55k work truck uninsured, that's your money. Get the quote
      first. Don't email dealerships until you can send a COI the same day.
      Never write "insured" in an email until it's true.
- [ ] **Your fleet price.** Businesses expect a lower per-vehicle price than
      retail, in exchange for volume and no selling. Decide your floor before
      anyone asks: your retail full detail pays about **$74 to $150 an hour**
      ($369 over 5 hours up to $449 over 3). Pick the lowest hourly you'll
      accept, times the hours a vehicle takes you. That's your number. Your
      existing Clean Club price ($125 a visit) is a ready-made maintenance
      price for vans after the first full detail.
- [ ] **How many fleet slots you'll give up.** Your week holds about 7 jobs
      right now (one weekday afternoon slot, two on Saturday). Fleet work pays
      less per car but saves driving (six vans in one lot, one setup). Decide
      how many can go to it before a dealer asks for 20 a week. Don't mention
      capacity in the emails.
- [ ] **Water and power.** Same rule as a driveway: they provide a spigot and
      an outlet. Business lots almost always have both. The emails say so up
      front so nobody is surprised.
- [ ] **Where the soapy water goes.** Washington cities don't allow wash
      water down storm drains, because it runs straight to the creeks with no
      treatment. On a paved commercial lot, a facilities person may ask. Know
      your answer (wash where it runs to grass or gravel, a containment mat,
      or rinseless for maintenance washes).
- [ ] **A mailing address for the footer.** Federal law (CAN-SPAM) requires a
      physical postal address in every sales email, B2B included. If you
      don't want your home address in strangers' inboxes, a USPS PO box or a
      UPS Store box both count.

## 2. Who to send to

Only the twelve towns: Snohomish, Lake Stevens, Everett, Monroe, Mill Creek,
Marysville, Bothell, Duvall, Mukilteo, Woodinville, Granite Falls, Arlington.

**Not Lynnwood or Edmonds.** Most of the dealerships near you sit on the
Lynnwood/Edmonds stretch of Highway 99. You've said no to those towns, so
those dealers are off the list no matter how many cars they have.

| Type | Why they fit | Who to email |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Service companies** (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, landscaping, pest control, cleaning, home health) | Owner reads their own email. Vans sit in customers' driveways all day. Best fit for one guy. | The owner. Name is usually on the About page. |
| **B. Independent used-car lots** | No detail department. Every trade-in needs one. | The owner or manager. |
| **C. Franchise dealerships** | They have a detail department or a recon vendor already. Pitch **overflow**, not replacement. | Used Car Manager or Pre-Owned Director. Call the front desk and ask who handles recon; `info@` goes nowhere. |
| **D. Employers with a full lot** | Costs them nothing. Puts you in front of 50+ people. | Office manager or HR. |

A realistic list in twelve towns is one or two hundred businesses. You don't
need a blast tool for that.

## 3. What the research says (why the emails look like this)

- **Short wins.** 50 to 125 words gets the best reply rates; past 200 words it
  roughly halves. The top senders average under 80.
- **Ask for interest, not time.** Gong looked at 304,174 cold emails. "Worth
  trying on one truck?" beats "Are you free Tuesday at 4?" at the cold stage,
  because giving up time feels like a loss. Once they reply, flip it and name
  a day.
- **Follow up.** About 4 in 10 replies come from follow-ups, not the first
  email. Stopping after one leaves those on the table.
- **Short, plain subject lines**, about 4 or 5 words, that say what the
  email is about. No tricks.
- **A true first line about them.** It's the only part that tells them this
  isn't a blast. **It has to be true.** Don't write "I drove past your yard"
  unless you did.
- **Plain text.** No images, no attachments, one link at most (your site in
  the signature). That's what keeps it out of spam.
- **Send by hand, 10 to 20 a day, Tuesday to Thursday morning.** A blast from
  Gmail gets reported as spam, and once more than 0.3% of your emails get
  reported, Google starts filtering or rejecting your mail. At 15 a day you cover the whole list in two or three
  weeks. Skip the last few days of the month for dealerships: that's when
  they're pushing to hit numbers.
- **The laws:**
  - CAN-SPAM: honest From line, honest subject, say it's a sales email, a
    postal address, a way to opt out, and honor it within 10 business days.
    Fines run over $50,000 per email. The footer below covers it.
  - Washington's own law (RCW 19.190) makes **any** misleading subject line
    illegal when the recipient is in Washington. So: never a fake "Re:" or
    "Fwd:" on a first email, and no fake deadlines. The Friday to Monday
    offer stays out of these.

## 4. The first email

Fill in the brackets. Delete any bracket you can't fill truthfully.

**Footer, on every email in this file:**

```
Mikey's Mobile Detailing, [PO box or street address], Snohomish, WA 98290.
This is a sales email from a local business. If you'd rather not hear from
me, reply "stop" and I won't email you again.
```

**Signature:**

```
Mikey
Mikey's Mobile Detailing
(425) 600-7897 | mikeysdetailing.com
```

### A. Service companies (vans and trucks)

Subject: `detailing the [Company] vans`

```
Hi [First name],

[True first line: "I'm in [Town] most weeks and see your vans around."]

I'm Mikey, a mobile detailer based in Snohomish. Your trucks park in
customers' driveways all day, so they're the first thing people see before
your tech knocks. I can detail them right in your yard, so nobody loses
work hours at a car wash. All I need is a spigot and an outlet.

300+ cars since 2021, 5.0 on Google across 41 reviews. You pay after, and
only if it's right.

Worth trying on one truck?
```

### B. Independent used-car lots

Subject: `detailing trade-ins on your lot`

```
Hi [First name],

[True first line: "I saw you've got about [40] cars listed right now."]

I'm Mikey, a mobile detailer based in Snohomish. When a trade-in comes in
rough, I can detail it on your lot so it's ready for photos without leaving.
Stains, odors, pet hair: the stuff a buyer notices the second they open the
door. All I need is a spigot and an outlet.

300+ cars since 2021, 5.0 on Google across 41 reviews. If it's not
front-line ready, you don't pay.

Want to try me on the next one?
```

### C. Franchise dealerships (overflow)

Only once you're insured. Subject: `overflow detailing for [Dealer]`

```
Hi [First name],

When your detail department gets backed up (month-end, a wave of trade-ins,
someone out sick), what happens to the extra cars?

I'm Mikey, a mobile detailer based in Snohomish. I can come to your lot and
take the overflow when you need it. Call me when you need me, no minimum.
All I need is a spigot and an outlet.

300+ cars since 2021, 5.0 on Google across 41 reviews.

Worth keeping my number for the next backup?
```

### D. Employers with a full lot

Subject: `a perk for your team`

A full detail is 3 to 5 hours, so a "detail day" is two or three cars, not
twenty. The value is the fifty people who see you working. Pitch it that way
to yourself, not to them.

```
Hi [First name],

I'm Mikey, a mobile detailer based in Snohomish. I'd like to set up in your
parking lot on a workday and detail a few of your employees' cars while
they're at their desks. They book and pay me directly, so it costs you
nothing but a spigot, an outlet and a couple of parking spots.

300+ cars since 2021, 5.0 on Google across 41 reviews.

Would your team use that?
```

## 5. Follow-ups

Send these as a **reply in the same thread**, so your first email sits
underneath. ("Re:" is honest here because it really is a reply.) Stop the
moment anyone answers, yes or no.

**Day 3 or 4:**

```
Hi [First name], bumping this in case it got buried. Here are my Google
reviews if you want to see what people say: mikeysdetailing.com/reviews/

Same offer: one [truck / car], you pay after if it's right.
```

If you have a real before and after of a work van or a trade-in, link that
instead. It beats reviews.

**Around day 7.** For dealerships and lots, walk in between this email and
the last one (see section 7).

```
Hi [First name], one thing I left out: I work where the [trucks / cars] sit,
so nothing gets pulled off a job or moved to a bay. Want me to swing by and
take a look?
```

**Around day 14. The last one:**

```
Hi [First name], I'll leave it here so I'm not filling your inbox. If
[the trucks / a trade-in] ever needs it, I'm at (425) 600-7897.

Mikey
```

Then stop. Anyone who replied "stop" gets nothing more, ever.

## 6. When they reply

Now it's fine to name a day. They're interested, and the Gong data flips:
a specific time books more than a vague one.

**"How much?"**

```
Depends on size and condition, so I'd rather look than guess. A first full
detail is from $369, exterior only from $199. After that, keeping it up is
$125 a vehicle every 4 or 8 weeks. For [5] or more in one visit it's
[YOUR FLEET PRICE] each. Can I come look at them [day]?
```

Don't send this until you've filled in your fleet price (section 1).

**"Send me more info."** Don't send a brochure. Send the offer again, shorter:

```
Sure. I come to your lot, you provide a spigot and an outlet, I do the
[trucks / cars] there. You pay after, only if it's right. Easiest way to
see it is one [truck / car]. Want me to do one [day]?
```

**"Are you insured?"** Once you are:

```
Yes, general liability and garagekeepers. I'll send the certificate today
with [Company] listed on it.
```

Until you are, there is no good answer to this, which is why section 1 says
insure first.

**"We already have someone."**

```
Makes sense. Keep my number for when they're booked up: (425) 600-7897.
```

**"Not right now."**

```
No problem. Mind if I check back in [a couple of months]?
```

Then put a reminder in your calendar and actually do it.

## 7. Walking in (dealerships and lots)

Managers are on the lot all day. An email plus a face beats either alone.
Go mid-morning on a weekday, not a Saturday and not month-end:

> "Hi, I'm Mikey, I sent [name] an email last week about detailing on your
> lot. Wanted to put a face to it. Here's my card."

That's it. Leave the card and go.

---

## 8. Keeping track

One row per business in a sheet:

Business, Town, Type (A/B/C/D), Contact name, Email, Sent, Follow-up 1,
Walked in, Follow-up 2, Follow-up 3, Reply, Next step.

Once a month: how many sent, how many replied, how many did one vehicle, how
many came back. The last number is the one that matters.
