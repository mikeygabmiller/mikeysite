# Business cards: what goes on the front and the back

Research for the die-cut card in this folder ([README.md](README.md)), done from the web on 2026-10-01. Most business card advice comes from printers who sell cards, so read it as opinion unless a study is named. Statistics are labelled **verified** (I found the original data), **unverified** (repeated everywhere, origin not found) or **contradicted**. Each link says what kind of source it is.

## 1. The short answer

- **Front: who you are and how to reach you.** MOO's floor is "the name of your business and a way to get in touch" ([MOO, printer guide](https://www.moo.com/blog/inspiration/what-to-put-on-a-business-card)). Logo, business name and a short tagline is the printers' usual front; many real cards add the phone. Convention, never tested.
- **Back: one job, two or three things** ([4OVER4, printer guide](https://www.4over4.com/article/14-back-of-business-card-design-ideas-worth-stealing)). Printers list a dozen options. No study compares one back against another.
- **For strangers, the back's job is "get back to me":** the phone, a QR to one page that says what you get, and where he works. Mikey's back already does that.
- **Only two back patterns have real evidence, and both work on existing customers:** a punch card with a head start (34% finished it vs 19%, at a car wash) and an appointment line the customer fills in (18% fewer no-shows). Neither helps with a stranger.
- **Real detailer cards** carry contact details, a services list, a social handle and photos of their own work. Some printed discounts.
- **The famous keep/toss numbers are unverified.** "88% thrown away within a week" and its cousins lead back to a 2016 guest post by a card printer on Adobe's blog, citing nothing, and to a stats website that no longer exists.
- **The little real data:** in a 2012 Ipsos survey, 75% of US adults said they keep cards they're given at their desk (no "throw away" answer was offered). People keep branded items for quality and usefulness (ASI, 2019).
- **QR codes:** most US adults have used one, but nothing independent measures business-card scans. In a 7,500-person Gallup mail test only 4% of respondents used the QR code, and a QR-only invitation got fewer people online than a printed web address. Print both.
- **Ratings matter to strangers:** 68% of US consumers only use local businesses rated 4 stars or more, and most check on Google ([BrightLocal 2026, survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)).
- **The biggest fixable weakness on Mikey's card is small type, not content.** The town list and "in 60 seconds" are 5.4 to 5.6 pt, under every printer's 7 to 8 pt minimum.

## 2. Front: what cards contain

**Printers** (opinion): Vistaprint and UPrinting both put the logo, business name and an optional tagline on the front, and your name, contact details, website and social handles on the back. Vistaprint wants the business name to be "the largest piece of text on your card" ([Vistaprint](https://www.vistaprint.com/hub/business-card-information-essentials), [UPrinting](https://www.uprinting.com/business-card-information.html)). 4OVER4 asks for "one phone number" and "A tagline, only if it says something specific" ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/guide/top-10-tips-on-what-to-include-on-a-business-card)).

**Templates:** Vistaprint's Car Wash & Detailing gallery (28 designs) previews every design with the back set to blank. In the 10 distinct designs I opened, everything sits on the front: business name, "company message", full name, job title, address, phone, email, web, and on one a fax line ([template gallery](https://www.vistaprint.com/business-cards/standard/templates/automotive-transportation-industry/car-wash-detailing)). MyCreativeShop's 13 auto detailing designs are two-sided (section 6 has what their second sides carry); its "Mobile Service" design leads with MOBILE AUTO DETAILING and "Showroom results at your doorstep." ([template gallery](https://www.mycreativeshop.com/templates/business-cards/auto-detailing))

**Real detailers:** Unique G's Auto Detailing (Visalia, CA) put the owner's photo and business name on the front ([design brief, 2018](https://www.designcrowd.com/jobs/job/3264616)); Slick's Detail Shop (Spokane, WA) put logo, address, phone and email there ([design brief, 2018](https://www.designcrowd.com/jobs/job/3429029)). Detailers' advice to each other: "use a car that you detailed if you put one on there" ([Autopia forum, 2009](https://www.autopia.org/threads/business-card-ideas.27852/)) and "Emphasize you're mobile & will come to their location" ([AutoGeek forum, 2013](https://autogeekonline.net/threads/what-are-the-key-details-to-be-included-on-a-business-card.65139/)).

**Evidence:** none for any front layout. It's convention.

## 3. Back: what cards contain

The clearest framework is 4OVER4's. A back does one of three jobs: "Retrieval gets the person back to you: a QR code, a short URL, a handle. Proof shows what you make [...]. Retention gives them a reason to keep the card: a discount, a stamp grid, an appointment slot, a reference chart." Then: "Pick one. A back that tries to do all three ends up as a small grey block of everything", and "Five bullets, a QR code, a testimonial, a map, and your social handles at once is too much, so pick two or three." ([4OVER4, printer guide](https://www.4over4.com/article/14-back-of-business-card-design-ideas-worth-stealing)) Vistaprint: "You don't need to fill every inch of your business card to make good use of it." ([Vistaprint, printer guide](https://www.vistaprint.com/hub/design-ideas-back-business-card))

**The patterns with data behind them:**

- **Punch card (verified, for regulars).** In 2004 a car wash handed out 300 loyalty cards: half needed 8 stamps, half needed 10 but came with 2 already stamped. 34% of the head-start cards were redeemed vs 19%, and those customers came back 2.9 days sooner between washes ([Nunes & Drèze 2006, peer-reviewed](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)). Every redemption came within about five months, from people buying washes often. Detail customers come a few times a year, and a stranger has nothing to stamp.
- **Appointment line (verified, small study).** Two NHS practices that had patients write their own appointment details on the card saw 18% fewer missed appointments than their previous six-month average ([Martin et al. 2012, peer-reviewed, before/after](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308641/)). It only helps after a booking, and Mikey's booking already texts a confirmation and two reminders (repo `CLAUDE.md`).
- **Ratings (verified, indirect).** BrightLocal 2026 (1,002 US adults): 97% read reviews for local businesses, 68% only use one rated 4.0 or more, 31% need 4.5, 47% won't use one with fewer than 20 reviews, 71% check on Google ([survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). The caution: for products sold online, purchase likelihood peaked at ratings of 4.0 to 4.7 and fell toward 5.0, read as "too good to be true" ([Northwestern Spiegel Research Center 2017](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/04/Spiegel_Online-Review_eBook_Jun2017_FINAL.pdf)). Products, not local services.
- **Referral (verified, indirect).** At a German bank, referred customers left about 18% more slowly and averaged about 25% more margin, a gap that shrank over time (almost 10,000 accounts, 33 months; [Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte, authors' summary](https://www.marketing.uni-frankfurt.de/fileadmin/user_upload/dateien_abteilungen/abt_marketing/Bilder/Professor_Skiera/Publikationen/Do_Referral_Programs_Increase_Profits.pdf)). Nothing shows a printed card offer produces them.
- **Useful info (verified, adjacent).** US consumers keep promotional items for quality (52%), usefulness (48%) and looks (36%) ([ASI 2019, trade-association survey](https://media.asicentral.com/resources/Global-Ad-Impressions-Study.pdf)). Pens and bags, not cards, from an industry that sells them.
- **Handwritten note (adjacent).** A handwritten sticky-note request roughly doubled survey returns, 76% vs 36%, in Garner's 2005 study, as reported by the UK's Behavioural Insights Team ([BIT blog](https://www.bi.team/blogs/paper-of-the-week-using-post-it-notes-to-increase-survey-responses/)). I couldn't open the paper (403).

**What real data there is:**

- **Ipsos, 2012** (online, US adults 21+, 702 people plus 305 small business owners, July 30 to August 8, 2012; the PDF is on MOO's site). Asked what they do with cards they receive: 75% keep them at their desk to reference later, 25% scan them, 17% pin them up, 17% use them for LinkedIn. There was **no "throw away" answer**, so it can't settle the 88% either way, and it's about networking ([Ipsos, survey PDF](https://www.moo.com/us/bit-bucket/survey.pdf)). **Verified.**
- **Ipsos, 2024** (1,089 adults, Great Britain): only 23% have ever handed out a physical card, and the main reason non-users give is no need (71%) ([Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/the-death-of-the-business-card)). Cards are a trade tool, not an everyday habit. **Verified.**
- **Vistaprint and Wix, 2025** (1,000 US small business owners): 58% use business cards; 67% in home services and trades, their top traditional tactic ([printer-funded survey](https://small-business-marketing.vistaprint.com/strategies-in-motion)). **Verified** as their result.

Nobody has measured what makes a stranger keep a detailer's card. What there is points where common sense does: well made, useful, easy to act on later.

## 5. QR codes on cards

**Do people scan?** YouGov, June 2021 (1,200 US adults): 55% had used a QR code at a bar, restaurant, venue or store in the past three months; 14% found them difficult, 20% of those 65 and over ([YouGov, survey](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/36657-qr-codes-leaving-older-americans-behind)). **Verified**, but from 2021. A 2024 TEAM LEWIS survey put past-year use at 68%, as reported by [QR Code Generator](https://www.qr-code-generator.com/blog/call-to-action-qr-codes/); TEAM LEWIS's page returned 403, so **unverified by me**.

**From paper, many still type.** Gallup mailed 7,500 people a survey invitation in three versions (web address, QR only, both). Only 4% of respondents used the QR code, and with the QR as the only way online, 9.0% completed on the web vs 15.3% with a typed address ([Gallup methodology blog, 2018](https://news.gallup.com/opinion/methodology/241808/quick-response-codes-enhance-hinder-surveys.aspx); data from about 2017). The Census Bureau's 2024 test found all 20 people (average age 29.9) scanned a code easily ([Census Bureau report](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2024/adrm/cbsm/rsm2024-05.pdf)). People can scan; plenty would rather type.

**Business-card scan rates:** no independent data. "Business card QR codes boast a remarkable 34% scan rate" ([Supercode, vendor blog](https://www.supercode.com/blog/why-qr-code-business-cards-are-a-must)) cites a [Qrlytics page](https://qrlytics.eu/blog/qr-code-business-card-advantage) that doesn't contain the number; "87% of people find QR codes on business cards useful" ([Mobilo, vendor blog](https://www.mobilocard.com/post/qr-code-on-business-card-good-or-bad)) has no source. Both **unverified**. Mikey's code carries `utm_source=card`, so Google Analytics will count his real scans, which beats any of these.

**Best practice** (printer and vendor guidance, plus the QR standard):

- **One page that does one thing.** Vistaprint suggests booking pages; Jobber, for detailers: "include a QR code that links to your booking page" ([Jobber, software vendor](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/auto-detailing/car-detailing-advertising/)). In QRStuff's sample of 63,691 destinations, 82.7% were deep pages, not home pages ([QRStuff 2026, platform data](https://www.qrstuff.com/scan-report/)).
- **Say what they get.** "The CTA is not optional. A bare QR code with no label creates confusion and lowers scan rates." ([QRStuff, vendor blog](https://www.qrstuff.com/feeds/blog/qr-codes-business-cards-good-bad))
- **Size and margin.** Vistaprint: "If you're using a dynamic QR code, we recommend a minimum size of 0.8″ x 0.8″ (20 mm x 20 mm) with around 0.2″ of clear space around it." 4OVER4: "About three quarters of an inch square is the practical floor on a printed card, and a full inch is safer. What matters more than size is the quiet zone". The standard: "QR Code requires a four-module wide margin at all sides of a symbol" ([DENSO WAVE](https://www.qrcode.com/en/howto/code.html)).
- **Shorter link, bigger squares.** More characters means more squares per side ([DENSO WAVE](https://www.qrcode.com/en/about/version.html)), so smaller squares at the same printed size, which old phones in poor light struggle with.
- **Dark on light, and test it** on several phones before ordering ([Vistaprint QR guide](https://www.vistaprint.com/hub/creative-ways-to-use-qr-codes)).
- **Static is fine here.** Vistaprint prefers dynamic codes so the link can change. Mikey's points at his own domain, so he can change the page without reprinting, as long as mikeysdetailing.com and its `#booking` section keep working.
- **Logos:** QR Code Generator's data (about 2 billion scans) found codes with logos got 53% more scans each ([QRCG 2026, platform data](https://www.qr-code-generator.com/blog/how-to-create-high-performing-qr-codes/)). **Verified** as their finding, but a correlation, not proof a logo causes scans.

- **AutoGeek forum**: one back listed services, ran a referral program on a back "you can write on", and said "Every business card is 20% off any Detail" ([thread, 2013](https://autogeekonline.net/threads/what-are-the-key-details-to-be-included-on-a-business-card.65139/)). Others: "less is more", "order a heavy stock", and nobody recommended printing prices ([thread, 2012](https://autogeekonline.net/threads/designing-my-detailing-business-card-help.46415/)).

**Templates.** On the second sides of MyCreativeShop's 13 designs: a services list on 5, "Book your appointment today" on 3, just logo, tagline and web address on 4, a QR code on 1. None has a punch card, rating, appointment line or service area, and the design named "Refer-a-Friend Program" shows no referral offer. 5 of the first 15 Zazzle mobile detailing designs have "QR Code" in the name ([Zazzle](https://www.zazzle.com/mobile+detailing+business+cards)). Detailing software maker Mobile Tech RX advises services, social handles, a call to action and "You don't want them to be too cluttered" ([vendor guide, 2020](https://www.mobiletechrx.com/resources/car-detailing-business-cards/)).

**What it adds up to:** detailers print contact details, services, a handle and their own work. None of the examples carried a town list or a rating. That makes Mikey's back unusual, and right for a card handed to strangers in a fixed area: their first two questions (do you come here, are you any good) are answered before they text.

## 7. What this means for Mikey's card

> **Applied on 2026-10-01**, after this was written: recommendations 1 (on the back; the front's two small labels are left for Mikey), 2 (0.8 in code, four-square margin, the link kept as it is), 3, 4, 8 (in `ORDERING.md`) and 9 (the die now rounds inside corners to 0.125 in). Left as Mikey's calls: 5, 6 and the front's small type. The numbers below describe the card as it was when researched.

**What the research supports. Keep these.**

- **No prices.** "A price dates the card faster than anything else on it." ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/what-should-you-not-put-on-a-business-card))
- **One number, "Text or call".** 4OVER4 says one number. In a January 2026 survey of 1,000 US consumers, 33% preferred to text a business with a question, 18% to call ([SimpleTexting, vendor survey; it sells texting](https://simpletexting.com/blog/texting-and-sms-marketing-statistics/)). Weak evidence, same direction.
- **"I come to you." and the twelve towns.** Specific, which is 4OVER4's test for a tagline, and the service-area point (opinion).
- **QR to the quote calculator, with what you get named and tracking on, plus the address in type.** Matches every guide (convention); the Gallup test is the evidence for printing the address too.
- **Thick stock.** The "quality" statistics are unverified, but ASI puts quality first among reasons people keep branded items (adjacent).
- **Dropping the appointment line and glovebox tips.** The only evidence-backed back patterns work on existing customers. For a stranger card, the research backs Mikey's call.
- **A shape inside 3.5 x 2 in.** 4OVER4 warns a shaped card "does not sit flat in a wallet" ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/creative-business-card-design-ideas-that-make-a-lasting-impression)); this one does. That shapes get kept is printer opinion.

**Where it's weak.**

| Town list (back) | 5.6 pt | 7 pt ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/guide/top-10-tips-on-what-to-include-on-a-business-card)); 8 pt ([Vistaprint](https://www.vistaprint.com/hub/design-ideas-back-business-card), [UPrinting](https://www.uprinting.com/blog/how-to-design-a-business-card/)) |

The QR prints about 0.76 in square (a 0.84 in white box minus 3 pt padding), under Vistaprint's 0.8 in, a figure it gives for dynamic codes, which hold short redirect links. Mikey's link is 69 characters, a 37 x 37 grid, so each square is about 0.52 mm. The white margin is about 2 squares wide against the standard's 4, with a beige outline just outside it.

The back holds five things (rating, phone, web address, towns, QR) against 4OVER4's "two or three", and two repeat the front. And "Snohomish County, WA" on the front doesn't cover all twelve towns: Duvall and Woodinville are in King County and Bothell is split ([Duvall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duvall,_Washington), [Woodinville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodinville,_Washington), [Bothell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bothell,_Washington), Wikipedia).

**Recommendations, most important first.**

1. **Bring the small type up to 7 pt at least, 8 if it fits**, the town list first: it's how a stranger in Duvall learns he comes to them. Opinion and convention, but every printer agrees ("if someone needs a magnifying glass, you have lost them", [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/14-back-of-business-card-design-ideas-worth-stealing)). That likely means three lines of towns instead of two; #3 frees the room.
2. **Give the QR more room.** Get the code itself to at least 0.8 in, more if the caption can sit beside it (the red bump sits right above the code, so there's little room to grow upward), widen the white margin to 4 squares (about 0.08 in at today's square size) and move the beige outline outside it. If it can't grow, encode a shorter link instead: `https://mikeysdetailing.com/card` makes a 29 x 29 grid, squares about 27% bigger at the same size, but needs a `/card` page that forwards to the calculator with the same `utm_source=card&utm_medium=print` tags (GitHub Pages can't redirect on the server). Either way, test with an old phone in dim light. Basis: the QR standard ([DENSO WAVE](https://www.qrcode.com/en/howto/code.html)) plus printer guides (Vistaprint, 4OVER4).
3. **Drop the "Online" row from the back.** The address is on the front ("Do not repeat the front", 4OVER4) and the QR covers it for scanners. Keep the phone on both sides; it's the card's whole job. Opinion.
4. **Keep the QR on the quote calculator with "See your price in 60 seconds", and bring "in 60 seconds" up to 7 pt or more.** It names the payoff, as QRStuff and Vistaprint ask (convention), and the 60 seconds is the hook, now set in the card's smallest type. After a few weeks, read card / print in Google Analytics: it's the only scan data that applies.
5. **Keep "5.0 on Google", knowing it can go stale.** People check stars, mostly on Google (BrightLocal, evidence). But if all 41 reviews are five stars, one 1-star review makes the average 4.90 (206 ÷ 42), and 500 cards then disagree with Google. "Read my reviews on Google" never goes stale but says less. His call.
6. **Consider "Snohomish, WA" for the front's location line.** It's his base and true for all twelve towns, so nobody in Woodinville assumes he won't come. Fact plus opinion.
7. **If space is left after 1 to 3, one more line at 7 pt or more:** a services line ("Interior · Exterior · Full Detail · Ceramic Coating · Paint Correction"), common on the detailer cards and templates above, or "No deposit. You pay when it's done.", which is true and never dates. No evidence for either. Opinion.
8. **Choose a writable matte back over soft-touch** (the README allows both), so he can jot a name or a quote when he hands one over. Vistaprint: "If you need to write on the card, look for an uncoated, matte or other writable finish". Opinion, with the handwritten-note study as adjacent evidence.
9. **Ask the printer about the die's inside corners.** The generator rounds them to 0.08 in (`CLOSE`); 4OVER4 wants "at least 0.125 in on any inside corner". One printer's spec.

**Leave off.**

- **Prices and any dated offer**, Rain-Ready included. The reason 4OVER4 gives for promo codes ("The deadline is the part that makes it get used") is why a card that lasts years can't carry one.
- **Hours**: they change when school ends. **Review count**: it grows. **"Licensed and insured"**: unconfirmed. **Lynnwood, Edmonds**: not served.
- **A punch card or appointment line on this run.** The evidence for both comes from people who already buy. An after-job card is a separate small run.
- **A referral reward.** Referred customers are worth having (Schmitt et al.), but a printed amount is a standing offer that can't change, and new offers are Mikey's decision first.
- **Any statistic from section 4.**
