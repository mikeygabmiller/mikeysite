Why nobody gets 100

Every panel on a car is easy. It's the eight inches around every panel that aren't. Watch where your score actually leaks out and you'll find the same four places every professional detailer loses time on:

  • The lower quarter of every panel. Road spray throws the worst of the dirt at the bottom six inches, which is exactly where a lazy arc from your shoulder doesn't reach.
  • Inside the wheel arches. Out of your line of sight, permanently filthy, and the first thing anyone notices when they crouch down.
  • Behind the mirror and around the door handle. Small shadowed pockets your pad physically cannot sit flat against.
  • The shut lines. The gaps between doors, hood and trunk hold dirt and throw it back out over paint you already finished.

The game only reproduces one thing honestly, and it's the thing people don't expect: the last 5% costs more than the first 80%. That's the entire economics of paint correction in one sentence.

Why the polisher streaks when you rush

A polishing pad has to stay against the paint long enough to actually cut. Whip it across a panel and it skips — it only partly levels the swirls, and it leaves curved buffer trails called holograms. In a garage they're invisible. In direct sun they're all anyone can see. The fix isn't more pressure, it's a slower arm and a pass that overlaps the last one by about half the pad.

Real correction is the same job at a tenth of the speed and with a lot more on the line. A full paint correction is several stages across every panel on the car, and taking too much clear coat off means a body shop and a repaint, not a restart. Once the swirls are out, a ceramic coating is what stops them coming straight back the first time someone runs it through an automatic wash.

How to play

Hold down the mouse button, or a finger on a phone, and drag across the car. Stage one is a pressure washer — the dirt comes off wherever the spray lands. Stage two is a dual-action polisher — the gloss comes up wherever the pad passes, provided you don't rush it. Both stages are timed and the score is simply how much of the car you actually finished.

The car and its dirt are generated fresh from the date, so everyone playing on the same day gets the same car and the same mess. Compare scores honestly.
