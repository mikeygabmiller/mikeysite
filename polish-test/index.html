    Why it's harder than it looks

    Polishing isn't scrubbing. The pad only cuts inside a narrow speed band — drag it too slowly and friction heats the clear coat until it lets go, push it too fast and the pad skips across the surface without finishing the cut, leaving curved buffer trails called holograms. Those trails are invisible indoors and painfully obvious in direct sun.

    The other half of the job is boring on purpose: every pass has to overlap the last one by about half the pad, edge to edge, for the whole panel. Miss that and you get stripes that only show up once the panel dries. This test scores exactly those four things — coverage, pass consistency, speed control, and heat.

    Real work is the same, just slower and with more at stake. A full paint correction runs several stages across every panel on the car, and a burn-through means a body shop and a repaint, not a do-over. If you'd rather the swirls came out and the clear coat stayed on, that's the job. Afterwards, a ceramic coating is what keeps them from coming straight back.
